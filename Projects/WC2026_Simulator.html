Module 5 · Model Methodology
How the model works
A Poisson regression approach empirically calibrated on 144 World Cup group stage matches (2014, 2018, 2022).
01
Compute team strength score

Weighted combination of 8 features: ELO rating (0.33), FIFA ranking (0.30), win rate (0.14), goals scored avg (0.10), defensive record (0.07), top-5 league players (0.03), GDP per capita (0.01), WC titles (0.01). Weights derived from logistic regression on WC 2014–2022.

02
Compute expected goals (xG)

xG = 1.35 × (attack strength / 1.7) × opponent defense factor. Home team gets +4% boost. Away team adjusted −3%. Floor of 0.3 xG prevents zero-probability scorelines.

03
Build Poisson scoreline probability matrix

Apply Poisson(xG) independently to each team across scores 0–6. Build a 7×7 joint probability matrix. Most probable cell = predicted score. Summing triangles gives win/draw/loss probabilities.

04
Knockout stage: no draws allowed

In knockout rounds, draw probability is redistributed proportionally to win/loss. The winner advances. Tournament champion probability = product of win probabilities along the entire predicted path.

Key findings from historical validation (2014–2022)

ELO is the strongest predictor: r = +0.44 with goal difference. Higher-ELO team wins 70.1% of matches.
GDP is real but weak: log(GDP) r = +0.13. Higher-GDP team wins only 51.4% — barely above a coin flip.
3-feature logistic regression: 68.8% accuracy (5-fold CV) on 144 historical group stage matches.
